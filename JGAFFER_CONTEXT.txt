--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE NAME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

JGaffer = Java Manager.

The user IS the manager — assisted by Java's brain. Not a stats consumer.
Not a puzzle solver. A manager who understands why their team plays the way
they play.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A casual football fan watching a match sees 22 players moving around a pitch
and feels the emotion — the goal, the miss, the tackle — but has no framework
to understand the intelligence behind it.

They don't know that the reason the goal happened was set up 15 passes ago by
a rotation the manager drilled in training for three weeks.

The tools that exist today serve two audiences that aren't the casual fan:
  - Stat dashboards (Sofascore, FBref) — built for analysts who already speak
    the language
  - YouTube breakdowns — passive, 15 minutes long, forgotten by next match

Nobody is building for the fan in the middle — the person who watches every
game, has strong opinions, but has never had the chance to feel what it is
like to make a tactical decision.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE VISION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Providing an expert guide to a casual fan."

JGaffer's job is to close the gap between emotion and understanding — not by
teaching tactics as a subject, but by showing the fan the manager's intention
in real time, at the moment it is unfolding, in language they already
understand.

The fan does not become an analyst. They become someone who watches the same
game and sees more of it.

One sentence version:
"The Duolingo for Football Tactics."

Duolingo does not give you a dictionary. It makes you play a game to learn a
language. JGaffer does not give you a stat dashboard. It makes you experience
a decision to learn the language of the pitch.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE USER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A Premier League fan who:
  - Watches every weekend
  - Uses Twitter/X during games
  - Has strong opinions about their club
  - Could not explain what a "half-space" is
  - Paid 12 euros for a ticket and wants to appreciate the football on display
    — not just be frustrated that their team is not scoring

They feel the game fine. What they cannot do is answer WHY things happen.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE CORE INSIGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every player on a football pitch has an expected position — where their role,
formation, and game situation says they SHOULD be.

At any given moment there is a delta between where they are and where they
should be. That delta is not random noise. That delta is the tactical story.

  - A defender 5 metres ahead of where he should be → space behind him
  - A midfielder 3 metres too narrow → the wide channel is open
  - A striker not making the run his formation demands → the attack breaks down

The casual fan watches the ball. The actual game is happening in those deltas.

"Should be" means: how well the players are implementing the manager's system.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE MANAGER DNA CONCEPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every manager has a tactical fingerprint. Not just a formation — a behaviour.

Examples:
  - Luis Enrique (PSG): fluid front-three rotations. Dembele starts as CF but
    drifts to RW. Kvaratskhelia moves to CF. Doue takes the left. These
    rotations are designed, not random.
  - Pep Guardiola (Man City): inverted fullbacks, high defensive line,
    goalkeeper as passing option, half-space occupation.
  - Jurgen Klopp (Liverpool): gegenpressing trigger within 5 seconds of losing
    the ball, vertical transitions, high energy pressing in wide areas.

The manager's DNA is:
  1. A historic, predominant way of playing
  2. Tactical instructions given to players — strict discipline OR freedom
  3. Observable in positional and event data across enough matches
  4. The thing that makes unusual player positions make sense

The fan who understands this watches Enrique's front three rotate and thinks
"oh — that is the plan" instead of "why is Dembele on the right?"


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE DATA REQUIRED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The most important data is SPATIAL — not statistical.

What is needed:
  1. Freeze frame positions — all 22 players + ball at the exact moment a
     decision is made (pass played, shot taken, run started). Not averages.
     Not heatmaps. Snapshots at decision moments.

  2. Sequential event chains — the 5-10 events leading to a moment. Goals and
     chances do not happen in isolation. The story starts several passes ago.

  3. Pressure context — distance of nearest defender, direction they are
     coming from, time the player had on the ball. Turns "bad decision" into
     "actually that was almost impossible."

  4. Formation and role data — what shape is each team in, what is each
     player's designated role in the manager's system.

  5. Manager behaviour profiles — observable tactical patterns inferred from
     tracking data across multiple matches.

What is NOT needed:
  - Season-long heatmaps (averages hide the specific moment)
  - PPDA, progressive carries, xA (nobody outside analytics Twitter knows
    these)
  - Transfer values, wages (different product entirely)

Best available data source: StatsBomb 360 open data (freeze frames of all 22
players at every event — exactly what is needed).

The key principle: humans understand geometry instantly. Show them where 22
people are and where the gap is and they understand without needing football
knowledge. The data job is to make that geometry visible.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THE PRODUCT JOURNEY (VERSION HISTORY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

V1 — Tactical Recommendation Engine
"Tell me what to do, manager."
Problem: You cannot recommend tactics to someone who does not speak the
language yet. Like giving someone a recipe in French when they have never
cooked.

V2 — Learn Through Puzzles (current build)
"Let me teach you the language first."
Problem: The puzzles are still abstract. A node graph of passing routes does
not feel like football. The fan is learning ABOUT the game, not experiencing
it.
What was built: RAG pipeline, HistGradientBoosting escape probability model,
Break the Press puzzle, playstyle classification, result screen with real-
world team equivalents and pundit commentary.

V3 — The Manager's Mind (target)
"Now you understand why the manager does what he does — and you can see it
happening in real time."
The fan is not learning tactics in isolation. They are watching Enrique and
suddenly thinking "oh — that rotation — I know why he set that up."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. THE PRODUCT LOOP (NON-NEGOTIABLE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every feature must fit this four-step loop. If it does not fit, cut it.

  Step 1 — THE HOOK (Moment)
  "Did you see that Arsenal goal yesterday?"

  Step 2 — THE CHALLENGE (Mission)
  "Most people missed the run Saka made to drag the defender away. Can you
  see the space he created?"

  Step 3 — THE ACTION (Interaction)
  The user makes a decision — moves a player, picks a pass, identifies a gap.
  Something active. Not passive reading.

  Step 4 — THE REWARD (Understanding)
  "Spot on. That is called a decoy run. Arteta drills this every week in
  training. Here is how it opened the goal."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. THE SUCCESS CONDITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A user goes from the home screen to a genuine "aha" moment in under 60
seconds and says: "Oh — I get why they did that now."

Not "cool graphics." Not "interesting stats." That specific sentence.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. WHAT NEEDS TO BE REWORKED (CURRENT PRIORITIES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. DATA LAYER
     Understand and design the schema required to encode manager DNA.
     Research one manager deeply before building the system to store them.
     StatsBomb 360 freeze frames are the target data source.

  2. FLOW
     The time-to-meaning is too long. Too many steps before the fan reaches
     the moment that matters. Target: one sentence, one visual, one decision.

  3. THE INTERACTION
     Pick ONE interaction that perfectly expresses the vision. Not three puzzle
     types. One. Go deep on it.

  4. DATA SWAP (not a full rewrite)
     The RAG pipeline stays — point it at better data.
     The ML model (escape probability) likely goes — wrong question.
     The three-layer architecture (React / Spring Boot / FastAPI) stays.
     The data changes.

  5. BACKEND-FIRST PRINCIPLE
     The backend sends meaning. The frontend renders it.
     If a developer reads the JSON and immediately understands the tactical
     story being told — the UI will make sense.
     Formation coordinates and shift logic should NOT live in the frontend.

  6. CLARITY
     For every feature: who is confused without it, and who understands
     because of it? If you cannot name a specific person in a specific moment,
     the feature is not clear yet.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. WHAT TO BUILD NEXT (ORDERED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before any code:
  a. Pick one manager. Study them until you can write down 5 observable
     tactical behaviours.
  b. Confirm StatsBomb 360 as the data source. Understand the freeze frame
     schema.
  c. Design the one core interaction on paper. Not in code. On paper.

Then code:
  d. Build the manager DNA schema around what you learned in step (a).
  e. Swap the data source. Keep the pipeline architecture.
  f. Build one interaction end to end — not a prototype, a working thing.

Then:
  g. Rebuild the flow around that one working interaction.

14. THE QUESTION TO ASK BEFORE BUILDING ANY FEATURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Does this help a casual fan understand one specific thing that happened in a
real match — faster than watching a YouTube video?"

If yes — build it.
If no — cut it.
